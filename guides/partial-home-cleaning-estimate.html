CLEANING QUOTES / SCOPE

How to quote a partial-home cleaning job

When a customer requests just the kitchen and bathrooms, whole-home square footage isn't a useful measure of the work you're actually performing.

Start with the requested scope

Make a list of included rooms and the exact tasks. A kitchen exterior wipe-down differs from cleaning inside an oven. Bathrooms can vary drastically by fixtures, condition and buildup. Clarifying these details protects both the business and customer from surprises.

  • Count the included rooms or task areas.
  • Record what is and is not included.
  • Estimate person-minutes required per unit of work.
  • Note condition and whether this is the first visit.
  • Add supplies, access restrictions, parking, and travel considerations.

Convert the scope into labor

Example only: 55 person-minutes for a kitchen and 40 minutes for each of two bathrooms gives 135 minutes, or 2.25 person-hours. An owner might then add a contingency for uncertainty and paid travel/setup time. The actual estimate must reflect their crew and observed production rates.

A useful distinction

A two-person crew completing 2.25 person-hours of on-site work would take approximately 1.125 elapsed hours, not 2.25 hours each, if work divides perfectly. Paid travel and setup can change the final labor cost.

Price from total costs, not just wages

Calculate loaded paid labor, consumables, travel, allocated overhead, and payment processing. Then choose a margin target. If variable processing fees are p and target margin is m, a simplified price floor is (job costs + fixed processing fee) divided by (1 − p − m). This is a modeling approach, not a pricing standard.

Put the same scope on the quote

The estimate should make included rooms, quantities, exclusions, potential change orders, and price clear to the buyer. Keep your internal wage assumptions and target profit out of the customer-facing version.
